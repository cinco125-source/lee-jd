I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Aerospace Engineering at **KAIST**, where I completed my Ph.D. under Prof. Hyochoong Bang. My work sits between **data-driven methods and flight control** — I build the estimators and controllers that keep aircraft flying when something goes wrong, and take them from theory onto real flight hardware.

Much of my research develops **data-driven modeling and control** using sparse identification (SINDy), the Koopman operator, and Gaussian processes, applied to **fault detection, diagnosis, and fault-tolerant control** for multirotors, eVTOL, and urban air mobility. More recently I work on **navigation in GNSS-denied environments**, fusing quantum magnetometers and magnetic-anomaly maps through factor graph optimization so a vehicle knows where it is when satellites go quiet.

Across both threads I care about getting algorithms onto real **GNC systems** — redundant flight-control computers, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and flight campaigns on platforms from quadrotors to hoverbikes.

I have authored **21 international journal articles** and more than **70 conference papers**, hold **5 patents**, and have received over **ten best-paper awards** at venues including KSAS, ICROS, SASE, and ICCAS. I'm always glad to hear from people working on hard estimation, control, and navigation problems.
